# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

One household: the primary user and their spouse, managing their own family finances together. No other users, no multi-tenancy, no sharing across families. They use it periodically (monthly overview, ongoing annual budget upkeep) to stay on top of income, planned expenses, tithe/charity obligations, savings funds, and debts.

## Product Purpose

A private household finance tool for planning and tracking an annual budget, monthly income and expenses, religious tithe (מעשר) obligations and donations, savings funds, and a debts ledger. Success is having full control over the household's money and knowing everything is organized: what's budgeted, what's been used, what's still owed in tithe, and what the household owns or owes.

## Positioning

The one place that always shows, at a glance, whether the household's money and tithe obligations are on track — so nothing is forgotten and nothing needs to be reconstructed from memory.

## Brand Personality

Warm, calm, like a gracious host — this is a family's own home for their finances, not a corporate dashboard. Precision matters (tithe accounting must be exact and trustworthy), but the tone stays gentle and unhurried rather than clinical or alarming.

## Anti-references

Positive reference given: monday.com's board view — chunky rounded shapes and a table/board structure with colored group headers. This overrides the earlier "avoid SaaS admin panel" guess on visual boldness specifically; the household wants a livelier, more colorful, more structured look. Still avoid an anxiety-inducing finance app (aggressive red alerts, dense enterprise data-grid density) and still avoid literal multi-user/team collaboration chrome (permissions, assignees, @-mentions) — the board *look* is borrowed, not the team *function*.

## Design Principles

- Calm precision: numbers and tithe obligations must be exact and easy to verify at a glance, without feeling like an audit.
- Built for two, not a team: no multi-user chrome, permissions, or enterprise scaffolding — this is a household's private ledger.
- Warmth over corporate polish: the interface should feel like a well-kept home ledger, not a fintech product.
- Clarity first: status (covered / partial / pending / critical) must be legible instantly across annual budget, monthly overview, funds, and debts.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Reasonable baseline accessibility (readable contrast, keyboard-usable forms); no special requirements beyond that. The app is Hebrew-first and RTL by default.
